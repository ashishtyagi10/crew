# Loop — the inbox, a fifth five rounds

**Status: DONE (v0.27.73–77).** Set by the user after the fourth loop closed
(`2026-10-22-inbox-loop-4.md`): *"run the inbox enhancement loop 5 more times"*.

## Rules that hold every round

As in the loops before, which the user set:

- No feature the user did not ask for. A round fixes, simplifies or polishes what is there.
- `r` replies to everyone on the mail; there is no other reply, and no Bcc.
- The list, the open mail and the reply box are each a card, and the mail's card is always
  taller than the reply box.
- Every surface is judged on both pages; every file stays under the 200-line cap; the foot
  keeps every key visible; nothing is fetched that was not fetched before; no scratch file
  stays in the tree while `scripts/release.sh` runs.

## One round

1. **Walk a flow** the earlier loops did not, through its code and the pane it draws.
2. **Pick** what a reader would notice most, none of it a new feature.
3. **Build** it with tests that fail without it, release, and log the round below.

## Log

**Round 1 (v0.27.73): the unread count keeps up with you.**

- **The flow** (filing: `e`, `d`, `m`, `s`, `z`, and marking read as a thread opens). Each
  shows at once and goes to Gmail behind it, and undo puts a row back where it was. But the
  unread count, in the pane's name (`inbox · 2 unread`) and on the nav's INBOX card, was read
  only with a page of the list: after opening an unread mail, or `m`, or archiving one, it
  stayed as it was until the next check saw the mailbox move, up to a minute later.
- **A filing that went brings the count back with it** (`worker.rs`, `filed`): one
  `labels.get`, the call the list already makes; when it fails the filing says nothing more.
- **Tests:** a relabel that went asks Gmail for the count and answers with it.

**Round 2 (v0.27.74): a reply waits out an ended sign-in.**

- **The flow** (Google ending the sign-in: a revoked grant, or a Testing app's seventh day).
  The pane goes back to the setup page and keeps the thread and the reply box, which is
  right: signed in again, you are back in the reply. But the box still took every key, so
  the setup page's own (1–5 for the console, Enter to sign in, Esc) went into the hidden
  box; a reply ended mid-send stayed `sending` for good; and a half-typed search did the same
  as the box.
- **The box takes keys, the send chord and pastes only when signed in**
  (`InboxPane::ready`); an ended sign-in lets a send in flight go back to the words and drops
  a half-typed search.
- **Tests:** a reply ended mid-send keeps its words and is not stuck; on the setup page Esc is
  the page's and no letter reaches the box; signed in again, typing goes on in the box.

**Round 3 (v0.27.75): a key waiting for a thread acts on that thread.**

- **The flow** (`a`, `r`, `f`, `t` pressed on a row of the list). The thread opens, and the key
  waits for its mails (`then`). But it waited for any thread's: press `t`, Esc before the
  mails came, open another thread, and its mails made the todo, for the wrong thread; `r` and
  `f` opened a box on it, `a` handed it to smith.
- **The waiting key carries its thread's id** (`then`, `handoff.rs`) and acts only when that
  thread's mails come; another's lets it go (`take.rs`).
- **A file dropped on the inbox with no reply box open** says how to attach one (`filedrop.rs`)
  instead of nothing.
- **Tests:** `r` on a row, Esc, another thread opened and loaded: no box, the key let go.

**Round 4 (v0.27.76): a reply on its way keeps its box.**

- **The flow** (Gmail's answers arriving after you have moved on). A reply sent shows
  `sending to …` and its box stays until Gmail says it went (`Got::Sent` closes it), but Esc
  twice still let the box go meanwhile. Open another thread, start a reply there, and the
  first send's `Got::Sent` closed the new box, its words with it.
- **While a reply is on its way, Esc says so** (`on its way — the box closes when Gmail says it
  went`) instead of letting the box go (`composekeys.rs`).
- **Tests:** two Escs leave a sending box; Gmail's answer closes it.

**Round 5 (v0.27.77): a thread filed elsewhere.**

- **The flow** (the open thread archived or deleted in Gmail on the web or a phone). The next
  check's page no longer had it, and the selection fell back to the top row: the mail
  card's `‹ 1 of 12 ›` named a place the thread did not have, and `n` (the row after the
  selection) skipped the thread that had taken its place.
- **The selection keeps its place** when its thread leaves the list (`take.rs`), the counter
  shows only while the open thread is the selected row (`readpos.rs`), and `n` from a thread
  that has left opens the one in its place (`advance.rs`).
- **Tests:** the open thread gone from a page: the selection stays, no count, `n` opens the
  thread in its place, and the count comes back.

## The loop, closed

Five releases, v0.27.73 to v0.27.77, none of them a new feature, each from a flow walked
through its code, most of them about what happens when Gmail answers after you have moved on:

- the unread count keeps up with your own filing;
- a reply waits out an ended sign-in, and the setup page keeps its keys;
- a key pressed on a row acts on that row's thread only;
- a reply on its way keeps its box;
- a thread filed elsewhere leaves no false count, and `n` goes on from its place.
