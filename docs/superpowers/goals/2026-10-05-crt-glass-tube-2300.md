# Goal — the CRT family becomes a terminal running in glass: a tube from the year 2300

**Status: IN PROGRESS** — Pillar 1 (the tubes get glossy glass) shipped in v0.25.44; Pillar 2
(electric text: a white-hot filament core, selections in the phosphor) in v0.25.45.

**Set:** 2026-10-05 by the user: *"transform CRT themes to change the background to liquid glass
(glossy effect) and font color is bright, so that they look futuristic, like terminal running in
glass, since text would be electric CRT theme would look like theme in year 2300."*

## Where the tubes stand

The CRT family is the four phosphors: green and amber in `crates/crew-theme/src/presets_crt.rs`,
blue in `crates/crew-theme/src/presets_crt_cool.rs`, and violet in
`crates/crew-theme/src/presets_crt_violet.rs`, each tuned by a `CrtStyle`
(`crates/crew-theme/src/crtstyle.rs`: scanline, glow, glow_radius, flicker). The 2026-08-04
holographic goal (`2026-08-04-crt-holographic-overhaul.md`) gave them real bloom in v0.12.2.
Then v0.13.5 pulled the luminous sheet back out (the flat-tube decree), and it never returned.
When liquid glass came back for paper and modern pages in v0.22.93, the tubes were left out on
purpose: `style_for` in `crates/crew-theme/src/glass.rs` returns an all-zero style for any
`Theme::is_tube()`, and `tubes_stay_flat_even_at_high` in `crates/crew-theme/src/glass_tests.rs`
pins that.

So in 2026 a CRT pane is phosphor text on a flat near-black slab, with a bloom around the
strokes. It reads as a 1985 terminal remastered, not as a terminal from the future. The text
already glows. The **surface** it glows on is what is missing.

THE GOAL, in one line: a CRT theme looks like **a terminal running inside a slab of glass**. The
panes are thick, glossy, liquid-glass panels lit from within by the phosphor. The text is
electric: bright, saturated, and haloed like current running through a filament. The page under
the glass has depth. A screenshot should read as a UI from the year 2300, not a retro filter.

## Pillar 1: the tubes get glass, and the glass is glossy

Repeal the tube opt-out in `style_for`. CRT gets its own liquid-glass recipe rather than a
borrowed one:

- **A tinted glass body.** The sheet is a cool, deep, phosphor-tinted slab (green glass for green,
  smoked amber for amber, cyan crystal for blue, orchid for violet), clearly visible over the page.
  It is not paper-dark's faint white lift.
- **Gloss.** A specular **highlight band** runs across the upper part of each panel, the curved
  reflection a real glass slab throws: a soft, wide, slightly angled sheen, brighter at the top
  rim and falling off down the card. It also needs a hairline **key rim** (the SDF-gradient rim
  the v0.22.93 sheet already draws) in a near-white tint of the phosphor, plus a faint **bounce**
  on the lower lip.
- **Thickness.** Edge **refraction-like darkening and brightening**: the band just inside the rim
  is denser (thicker glass seen edge-on) and lit by the phosphor, so the panel reads as a block
  with depth, not a film. A soft coloured **glow shadow** sits under the card (light leaking out
  of the glass onto the page), in place of paper's grey drop shadow.
- **Focus.** The focused panel's glass is clearer and brighter, and its gloss sweeps on focus (the
  existing `panecardglow.rs` ignition clock). Unfocused panels frost over and dim.

The tests flip from pinning flatness to pinning the new contract: a tube's glass alpha exceeds
paper-dark's, the highlight band is measurably brighter than the card's lower body, and the rim
carries the phosphor hue.

## Pillar 2: electric text

The ink is lit, not printed:

- **Brighter, more saturated phosphors.** Raise `term_fg` / `ink` toward the gamut edge for each
  phosphor (e.g. green toward a pure electric green, blue toward a hot cyan-white core). Keep
  `text_muted` / `hint_fg` clearly a step down, so the hierarchy survives the boost.
- **A white-hot core.** Glyph strokes render with a slightly desaturated, brighter core and the
  saturated colour in the halo, the way a real neon tube or overdriven phosphor looks. This is
  the bloom chain's job, tuned per phosphor: a tight inner halo on the text plus the existing
  wide one on the frames.
- **Glass under text.** Text sits *on* the glass, so the bloom must read through the sheet and
  not be muddied by it. The glass tint may never pull the text toward the page colour.

Contrast is non-negotiable: the WCAG floors arbitrated in `crates/crew-theme/src/contrast.rs` hold
for every phosphor against its new glass-over-page background, and the OS high-contrast setting
still scales the effect back.

## Pillar 3: a page with depth under the glass

The page beneath the panels stops being a flat black void:

- A deep **gradient field** in the phosphor's family (the modern backdrop's wash and lattice,
  already shared by every theme through `ModernStyle`), dimmer than on modern themes but present,
  so the glass has something to refract and the gloss has something to sit over.
- **Scanlines move onto the glass**, if they are kept at all: they become a fine etched texture
  inside the panel, not a raster over the whole window. The future does not need a raster, so a
  phosphor may drop them entirely.
- The backdrop's ambient motion (the swirl) runs under the glass, so the panels look like they
  float over a living field.

## Pillar 4: proven on pixels

Every claim gets a headless measurement, the way the 2026-08-04 goal did it:
`crates/crew-render/tests/glass_headless.rs` and `crates/crew-render/tests/crt_headless.rs` grow
assertions for the highlight band, the rim hue, the glow shadow and the text core. A contact
sheet of all four phosphors, before and after, lands with the first release. Paper and modern
themes stay pixel-identical: this goal is CRT-scoped.

## Done means

1. `style_for` gives every tube a liquid-glass style. `tubes_stay_flat_even_at_high` is replaced by
   a test pinning tube glass that is stronger than paper-dark's, with a visible highlight band.
2. Each of the four phosphors ships its own glass tint, rim and glow-shadow colour, plus a brighter,
   more saturated `term_fg` / `ink`.
3. Text has a white-hot core and a coloured halo, measured headless: the stroke centre is brighter
   and less saturated than its first halo ring.
4. The page under CRT glass carries a visible gradient field and the ambient swirl. Any scanlines
   are confined to the glass or dropped.
5. Contrast floors hold for every phosphor (tests extended to the new glass-over-page background),
   and high-contrast mode scales the glass and glow back.
6. Idle CRT still renders a byte-identical frame twice in a row when ambient motion is off.
7. Paper and modern screenshots are pixel-identical before and after.
8. The user looks at a CRT theme and calls it futuristic: a terminal running in glass.
