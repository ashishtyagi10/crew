# Loop — the inbox, five more rounds

**Status: IN PROGRESS.** Set 2026-10-21 by the user: *"lets run the inbox enhancement loop 5 more
time - do not add unnecessary feature, reply -- should always be reply all I don't think we need
bcc, you can probably add border to each section and emal should always appear in bigger panel"*.

Asked what the last two meant, the user chose:

- **Borders:** the list, the open mail and the reply box are each a bordered card of their own.
- **Bigger:** the mail's card is always the tallest: the reply box never outgrows it ("it means
  mail panel should have tallest height").

## Rules that hold every round

- No feature the user did not ask for. A round fixes, simplifies or polishes what is there.
- `r` replies to everyone on the mail; there is no other reply, and no Bcc.
- The mail's card is always taller than the reply box.
- Every surface is judged on both pages (the Ink Reader light page and glass).
- Every file stays under the 200-line cap, and the foot keeps every key visible.
- Nothing is fetched that was not fetched before.

## One round

1. **Shoot** the list, a thread, the split view and the reply box (`inboxreadshot_tests`,
   `inboxsplitshot_tests`, `inboxmailshot_tests`) on both pages.
2. **Survey** them: what looks wrong, reads badly, wastes room, or takes a step too many.
3. **Pick** the two to four fixes a reader would notice most, none of them a new feature.
4. **Build** them with tests, shoot again, release (`scripts/release.sh`), and log the round.

## Log

**Round 1 (v0.27.58): the user's four asks.**

- **Reply is reply all.** `r` writes to everyone on the mail (its sender and the rest of its
  `To` in To, its `Cc` in Cc, never you); `R` and the box's other kind are gone, and agent
  smith's Gmail drafts address everyone too.
- **No Bcc.** The line, its key stops, its header and its help row are gone.
- **A card for each section** (`crates/crew-app/src/inbox/cards.rs`): the list, the open mail
  and the reply box each draw their own edge, a column of air from the pane's. The mail's
  card wears the subject on its top edge and `‹ 2 of 12 ›` at the right of it, so the two
  rows the subject took inside are the card's edges now, and the counter stays put while the
  mail scrolls. Every hit test (a thread, a star, a link, a folded mail, the counter, the
  box's lines and files) and every paint (the list's motion, a picture and its clip) asks
  `cards` where its card is.
- **The mail is always the tallest.** The reply box grows to a third of the pane and never to
  the mail card's height, however long the reply.
- Seen in the shots and left alone: a one-pixel break in vertical card edges at one row,
  where the harness's cell height is fractional; the renderer draws each cell's edge, and
  the reply box had the same before.
