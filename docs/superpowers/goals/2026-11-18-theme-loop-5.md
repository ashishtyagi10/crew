### Round 3 — a dark page's alarm reads louder than its caution (`darkread.rs`)
Behaviour: `readable::danger` (the LOAD figures past their limit, a failed
swarm worker, an inbox draft's tail, an invite's decline) kept
`(230, 90, 90)` at APCA Lc 38–40 on paper-dark, neon, prism, slate and
mercury and 51–55 on the other dark pages, while `warn` — the milder state
— drew at 64–70 everywhere and light pages drew danger at 61–70. Round 2's
lift is now one helper for both (`darkread::lift`), lifting from the HUE so
the high-contrast switch can only move a colour further from the page
(`asking_for_contrast_actually_moves_the_derived_colours` caught the first
version, which lifted the floored colour and came back one code dimmer
under AAA). Measured: danger Lc on 14 dark palettes 38.1–55.5 → 60.0–60.6
(`(230, 90, 90)` → `(255, 146–151, 141–146)`), hue within 12°; light pages
and tubes unchanged. Pinned: `darkread_tests.rs` (fails with the danger
lift bypassed). Rows: none.

Dropped: rank 3, the tubes' comments (Lc 27) and code (44) on the code
field. The tube ladder is lightness alone — prose ×1.8 code ×1.6 comment,
on one phosphor — and a lower field floor moved them by 1 Lc (measured
1.45 → 1.2: comment 26.9 → 27.8). Lifting them means collapsing the rungs
that make code and comments tell apart on a one-hue screen, the trade the
2026-11-15 loop declined too. It wants another axis (italic comments), not
another colour.

### Round 4 — soft and metal: a button is a raised key (`reliefbutton.rs`)

### Round 5 — soft and metal: the tab you are on stands up (`navreliefactive.rs`)

## Pending release

- **Quiet text on dark themes reads.** An unfocused pane's legend, the
  input bar's hint, the placeholder and the dash's small labels are spaced
  from the page by a contrast ratio, and the same ratio is a much smaller
  step to the eye on a dark page: every dark palette drew them at APCA Lc
  32–45, where the light palettes drew the same roles at 60–78, so they
  read as disabled. Dark palettes now have their own quiet rungs, about ten
  Lc brighter, still well under body text; light palettes and the CRT tubes
  are unchanged. Costs nothing per frame (the colours are fixed per
  palette). Idea: APCA's levels for secondary text, as the Ghostty and
  WezTerm theme discussions use for dark schemes.
- **Links on dark themes stand out.** A URL or a Cmd+clickable path was
  drawn in a fixed blue that, on paper-dark, neon, prism, slate and
  mercury, was dimmer than the blue the shell itself prints (APCA Lc 53
  against 62–69) — the one word asking to be clicked was the faintest
  coloured word on screen. On a dark page the link is lifted, in the same
  blue, to Lc 60, level with the light themes. Costs nothing per frame.
  Idea: APCA's body-text level; iTerm2 and Ghostty draw links in the
  palette's bright blue for the same reason.
- **Red means louder than yellow on dark themes.** The danger red — a LOAD
  figure past its limit, a failed swarm worker, a draft's tail — was drawn
  at APCA Lc 38–55 on dark palettes, dimmer than the yellow that only says
  "watch this" (64–70). It is now lifted, still red, to Lc 60 on every dark
  palette, as light palettes already drew it. Costs nothing per frame.
  Idea: APCA; GitHub's and Primer's dark themes brighten their danger
  foreground for the same reason.
- **Soft and metal themes: buttons are keys.** On porcelain, sand, mist,
  slate, mercury and silver every field is a well and every card is
  moulded, yet Save, Cancel and the to-do's "show N done" were flat bars
  of colour printed on the card. Each is now a raised key with its own
  soft shadow, its label on the key's face — Save in the theme's accent —
  so the one thing you press is the one thing that stands up. Clicks land
  exactly where they did. Costs one small sheet per button, only on those
  six themes. Idea: neumorphic controls (Dribbble's soft-UI kits, Apple's
  visionOS ornaments): a control stands proud of the surface it is on.
- **Soft and metal themes: the tab you are on stands up.** The nav sank
  every tab's card into its panel alike, so only the colour of a number
  said which tab was current. The active tab now rises out of the panel
  like a key while the others stay pressed in. Costs nothing new: the same
  sheet, raised instead of sunk. Idea: iOS's segmented control, a raised
  thumb on a sunk track.
