# Loop — the inbox, a seventh ten rounds

**Status: IN PROGRESS.** Set by the user after the sixth loop closed
(`2026-10-22-inbox-loop-6.md`): *"run the inbox enhancement loop 10 times"*.

## Rules that hold every round

As in the loops before, which the user set:

- No feature the user did not ask for. A round fixes, simplifies or polishes what is there.
- `r` replies to everyone on the mail; there is no other reply, and no Bcc.
- The list, the open mail and the reply box are each a card, and the mail's card is always
  taller than the reply box.
- Every surface is judged on both pages; every file stays under the 200-line cap; the foot
  keeps every key visible; nothing is fetched that was not fetched before; no scratch file
  stays in the tree while `scripts/release.sh` runs.
- Nothing a test does opens a browser, reads the real Downloads or the real grant.

## One round

1. **Survey** a part of the pane the loops before did not: its words, its failures, its
   look on every theme (`neon` is new since loop 6), its list at scale.
2. **Pick** what a reader would notice most, none of it a new feature.
3. **Build** it with tests that fail without it, release, and log the round below.

## Log

**Round 1 (v0.27.85): a check after new mail keeps the pages below and the place in them.**

- **The survey** (four at once, for the rounds to come: the inbox on all ten palettes, `neon`
  included, which drew clean; its words, against the code and the docs; its failures, traced
  from the network to the screen; and its list at scale, which gave this round). Any change to
  the mailbox (new mail, or a thread archived here) moves Gmail's history, and the next
  minute's check listed the first page again and put it in place of the whole list. Someone
  forty threads down, with the pages `j` brought in, was thrown back to the twenty-fifth
  thread, and the pages below were gone.
- **A first page listed again replaces only the threads it covers** (`motion.rs`,
  `refreshed`): the held threads older than its oldest stay below it, the selection stays on
  its thread, and a thread the page no longer carries leaves. Nothing is kept below a page
  Gmail says is the whole list.
- **`j` at the end goes on from the deepest page** (`worker.rs`, `went`): the check keeps the
  cursor of a list paged past its first page, and a further page adds only threads not held.
- **Tests:** fifty threads held, a new one on top: fifty-one, the selection still on the
  forty-first; a thread filed elsewhere leaves; the cursor rule; no thread twice.

**Round 2 (v0.27.86): mail going out answers for itself.**

- **The survey** (the failures traced in round 1). Any call's failure unlocked a reply box
  still sending: a check asked just before Cmd+Enter is answered first, and when it failed
  (a timeout, a busy Gmail) the box let go of a reply still on its way, so a second
  Cmd+Enter sent it twice, and words changed meanwhile went when the first one landed. And a
  forward counted as a load that nothing it answered ended: after one, the minute's check
  and `j`'s next page stopped for the rest of the session.
- **A send's failure is its own answer** (`Got::NotSent`, `worker.rs`): only it unlocks the
  box, and the box says "not sent" with Gmail's reason; any other failure leaves the box
  sending.
- **Mail going out is not a load** (`Ask::loads`, `asks.rs`), as a reply was not.
- **Tests:** a check failing mid-send leaves the reply sending, the send's own failure frees
  it with its words; after a forward the list is live.

**Round 3 (v0.27.87): a reply waiting out a sign-in is not lost to one Esc.**

- **The survey** (the failures). When Google ends the sign-in while a reply is being written,
  the pane keeps the box for after the sign-in, but shows the setup page, which never drew
  it, and Esc there closed the pane: the reply went with it, unsaid.
- **The setup page says the reply is kept** ("your reply to Ann Lee is kept: it opens again
  once you are signed in", `setup.rs`).
- **Esc there asks twice when a reply is kept** (`leave_setup`), as Esc does over words in
  the box: the first says the reply is kept and that Esc again discards it. With nothing
  kept, Esc closes the pane at once, as before.
- **Tests:** the kept reply is named on the page, survives the first Esc, goes on the second.

**Round 4 (v0.27.88): a thread that will not open says so.**

- **The survey** (the failures). When Gmail would not open a thread (a timeout, a 5xx,
  offline), its failure was any call's: the reader kept saying "loading…" with nothing to
  try, a later page could clear the note that said why, and an `r`, `f` or `a` pressed before
  it opened stayed armed for that thread.
- **A thread that does not open is its own answer** (`Got::Unopened`, `worker.rs`; the send's
  and this one share `failed_as`), and the reader says "could not open this thread" with
  Gmail's reason, wrapped, and "esc, then enter tries again", which it does (`reader.rs`).
  What was asked of it before it opened is let go.
- **Tests:** the reason in place of "loading…", nothing left armed, Esc and Enter ask again.

**Round 5 (v0.27.89): a refused archive or delete is said and put back, once.**

- **The survey** (the failures). When Gmail refused a filing (archive, delete, star, read),
  the worker sent the refusal and then the list again, and the list's page cleared the words
  in the same breath: the row came back with nothing said. The list only reached its first
  page, so a row filed further down did not come back at all; and the filing's undo stayed,
  so `z` added the row a second time.
- **A refusal is its own answer, after the list** (`Got::Refused`, `worker.rs`), carrying the
  thread: the row goes back where it was, as it was, the selection stays on its thread, the
  words say Gmail refused it and why, and the undo is let go (`undo.rs`, `refused`).
- **`z` never lists a thread twice**: a thread already back is selected, not added.
- **Tests:** a refused archive is back once with the reason, `z` has nothing to undo; `z`
  after a list that carried the thread selects it.

**Round 6 (v0.27.90): offline at the start, you are still "me".**

- **The survey** (the failures). The pane learns whose account it is once, from the profile
  the worker reads as it starts. Offline at that moment (a laptop waking before its Wi-Fi),
  the address stayed empty for the session: the list never said "me", and reply-all, which
  leaves you out by that address, wrote to you as well.
- **The account comes with the first list that reaches Gmail** (`workerlist.rs`, split from
  `worker.rs` with the list's paging), once, before its page. There is no thread to answer
  before there is a list, so no reply is written without it.
- **Tests:** a worker that never told the account tells it with its first list, and only
  then.
